# Youth soccer published activity candidates

Reviewed October 26, 2026. Seven concrete activity candidates were found in six first-party FIFA and FA publications. They are candidates for source-faithful board adaptations, not approved bundled lessons. No source establishes our mockup diagrams, a universal ability level, or automatic 7v7 and 9v9 versions.

The sources below are published on FIFA Training Centre or The FA's own coaching services. FIFA identifies its grassroots section as age-specific youth coaching resources; The FA labels its older session's age band and identifies the modern programme's age and coaches in its official session index. [FIFA grassroots collection](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots.php), [FA sessions index](https://learn.englandfootball.com/sessions), [FA session age label](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/england-dna-5-11-staying-on-the-ball).

All summaries below come from public written activity instructions read in full. No login-only content or embedded video was inspected. Publication dates are those shown by the publisher. Reuse/adaptation rights have **not** been cleared for any candidate; public access and attribution do not establish permission to bundle it.

### SRC01 Give and go

**Publisher/byline:** FIFA. **Published:** March 14, 2023. **Audience:** explicitly ages 8–12. **Direct source:** [Give and go](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/give-and-go.php).

**Relevant sections:** Skill development: Pass and move; Game application: Small sided game - Game related outcomes. The foot-passing activity uses numbered groups of up to six in a square and asks players to move after passing. The game uses up to 5v5 and requires a wide gate to be used before scoring, encouraging width and receiving options. The source provides easier/harder variants and questions for children. [Activity instructions](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/give-and-go.php).

**Sideline use and limits:** A01 and A02 could illustrate moving after a pass and the purpose of width. Retain the square/gate conditions if representing those activities. A generic triangular three-player diagram or a full-team 9v9 pattern would be our adaptation, not a published FIFA arrangement. Gates/square boundaries must be represented clearly; field markings alone are insufficient. The stated age band does not establish suitability for every child or skill level. **Rights:** unreviewed; **adaptation/coach review:** pending.

### SRC02 The art of keeping the ball

**Publisher/byline:** FIFA. **Published:** March 28, 2023. **Audience:** explicitly ages 8–12. **Direct source:** [The art of keeping the ball](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/the-art-of-keeping-the-ball.php).

**Relevant section:** Skill development: 6v6 possession game. Teams of up to six connect a chosen number of passes for a point; the activity progresses from hands to feet. Questions direct attention to using space, communicating and intercepting. The article also contains separate warm-up and eight-goal activities, which are not A03. [Possession activity](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/the-art-of-keeping-the-ball.php).

**Sideline use and limits:** A03 can illustrate players finding passing options in space. This source does not prescribe our proposed triangle layout or a fixed full-team formation. A board explanation is a selected moment from a live possession activity, not a replacement for its game and progression. **Rights:** unreviewed; **adaptation/coach review:** pending.

### SRC03 Playing the final pass

**Publisher/byline:** FIFA. **Published:** April 11, 2023. **Audience:** explicitly ages 8–12. **Direct source:** [Playing the final pass](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/playing-the-final-pass.php).

**Relevant sections:** Skill development: 6v6, ball to captain, 2 goals; Game application: 7v7, 2 goals. The first activity places 5v5 centrally and one target per team in the end zones; only that team's target can score and the role rotates. Its questions address reaching and supporting an available target. The later game allows up to 7v7 and encourages changing positions and goalkeeper turns. [Published setup and questions](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/playing-the-final-pass.php).

**Sideline use and limits:** A04 could illustrate a target moving into a passing lane. The 7v7 section supports player organisation and role rotation, not any particular 2-3-1 shape, exact starting positions, or 9v9 conversion. The game-application harder variations mention headed finishes; **exclude that variation from our first pack** and apply the target association's age-specific restrictions before selecting any variations. **Rights:** unreviewed; **adaptation/coach review:** pending.

### SRC04 Developing core skills with Clearwater Chargers U-10

**Publisher/byline:** FIFA; the session is delivered by Clearwater Chargers head coach Juan de Brigard. **Published:** June 3, 2025. **Audience:** explicitly U-10. **Direct source:** [Developing core skills with Clearwater Chargers U-10](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/global-grassroots-insights/u-10-clearwater-core-skills-1v1.php).

**Relevant section:** Part 2: 1v1 and 2v1 small-sided game. In a 30×40m area with mini-goals, an attacker first engages a defender. After crossing halfway, that defender stops and a second defender and supporting attacker become active, creating 2v1. On winning possession, the defender can combine in the opposite direction. Coaching points include recognising teammates and opponents after gaining an advantage. [Part 2 instructions](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/global-grassroots-insights/u-10-clearwater-core-skills-1v1.php).

**Sideline use and limits:** A05 can show the moment the supporting attacker becomes available and the choices around the second defender. Preserve the activation sequence if calling it an adaptation of this activity. It is a U-10 club session published by FIFA, not evidence of universal recreational readiness or a prescribed U12/9v9 tactic. A diagram needs practice boundaries and mini-goal context. **Rights:** unreviewed; **adaptation/coach review:** pending.

### SRC05 Marking and intercepting session defend as friends

**Published title in the official index:** Marking and intercepting session: defend as friends. **Publisher:** England Football Learning/The FA. **Coach:** Vinny Halsall, identified as an FA PE officer in the official index. **Published:** June 9, 2025. **Audience:** the linked six-week programme is explicitly for 7–11s in the official index. **Direct sources:** [Week-two session](https://learn.englandfootball.com/sessions/resources/2025/Marking-and-intercepting-session-defend-as-friends), [programme](https://learn.englandfootball.com/sessions/resources/2025/Session-programme-marking-and-intercepting-to-defend), [official age and coach listing](https://learn.englandfootball.com/sessions).

**Relevant section:** Defend as friends, third illustrated activity; also Week two in the programme. Three attackers occupy a channel's two ends and middle against two defenders. The attackers transfer the ball end to end; the defenders coordinate marking, cover and interceptions. [Written activity description](https://learn.englandfootball.com/sessions/resources/2025/Marking-and-intercepting-session-defend-as-friends).

**Sideline use and limits:** A06 could illustrate which defender challenges and which protects a passing route in that 3v2 setup. The 7–11 audience does not automatically cover 12-year-olds, and the activity does not establish a full-team pressing scheme. The session's web metadata misleadingly names a different week; use its visible activity heading, date and programme placement. The linked PDF could not be fetched; these findings rely on the accessible written page, not the PDF or video. **Rights:** unreviewed; **adaptation/coach review:** pending.

### SRC06 England DNA 5-11 staying on the ball

**Published title:** England DNA 5-11: staying on the ball. **Publisher/byline:** The Boot Room/The FA; no individual author shown. **Published:** April 4, 2019. **Audience:** explicitly ages 5–11. **Direct source:** [England DNA 5-11: staying on the ball](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/england-dna-5-11-staying-on-the-ball).

**Relevant sections:** Organisation; How to play. Paired opponents play 1v1 in two areas, with each colour defending the ball in one area and trying to regain it in the other. After a successful regain, the player carries the ball to their team's area. The session emphasizes creative possession under pressure and winning the ball before moving it. [Published activity instructions](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/england-dna-5-11-staying-on-the-ball).

**Sideline use and limits:** A07 offers a concrete youth transition example: regain, secure the ball and travel. It requires two clearly marked areas; it does not justify an arbitrary full-team counter-press, fixed transition pattern, or U12 extension. Our one-ball board would depict one pair's moment, not the complete multi-ball activity. **Rights:** unreviewed; **adaptation/coach review:** pending.

## Implications for the proposed library

- **Pass/support, width, possession options, a 2v1, paired defending and a simple transition have concrete youth source candidates.** Choose individual activities and preserve their purpose, age band and meaningful conditions.
- **Starting formation, a specific passing-triangle diagram, and goalkeeper build-out remain gaps in this activity review.** General curriculum support must not be substituted for a direct lesson/setup source. This is not a claim that no legitimate source exists.
- **No candidate is a cleared bundled item.** Before construction, attach the exact source section, a source-faithful setup description, intended audience, all adaptations, local-rule checks, recorded reuse basis, and qualified youth-coach review to the candidate. Later implementation/device acceptance is separate.
- **Do not promise eight lessons or two formats per lesson.** A source-native 3v2 exercise can remain a 3v2 explanation; selecting a 9v9 team elsewhere in the app does not validate expanding it to eighteen active markers.
