## Original board illustrations

The following cue and question wording is written for Sideline. Draw original geometric arrangements rather than copying source illustrations, screenshots or media. Keep original ages and activity size distinct from the app's 7v7/9v9 field preset. The illustrations select a teachable moment; they do not reproduce a complete live practice or enforce its rules.

### A01 Pass then move

- **Source:** FIFA, [Give and go](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/give-and-go.php), March 14, 2023; Skill development: Pass and move. Ages **8–12**. The foot-passing section uses numbered groups of up to six, a marked square, ordered passing and movement after release; cone-running conditions are progressions.
- **Original cue:** Release the ball, then find your next place.
- **Original question:** What space opens after your pass?
- **Board conditions:** A square boundary, numbered teammates, a ball and an open destination for the passer. A four-player example fits the source's group-size limit. Any displayed pass/run route is one illustration, not a required solution. Mark only the chosen progression; do not silently add an opponent.

### A02 Find width through a gate

- **Source:** FIFA, [Give and go](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/give-and-go.php), March 14, 2023; Game application section. Ages **8–12**, up to **5v5**. Two wide gates condition scoring; the ball can travel through a gate by a pass or a dribble.
- **Original cue:** Use width to make a route forward.
- **Original question:** Which gate gives us space to attack?
- **Board conditions:** Two visibly wide gates, goal direction and up to five markers per side. Retain the requirement to use a gate before scoring in the detail text. The full-pitch display is schematic, not a sourced full-team formation. Use number labels rather than imposing goalkeeper/defender roles absent from this activity's instructions.

### A03 Spread out to keep possession

- **Source:** FIFA, [The art of keeping the ball](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/the-art-of-keeping-the-ball.php), March 28, 2023; Skill development: 6v6 possession game. Ages **8–12**, up to **6v6**. Teams aim for a specified pass total; the session progresses from hands to feet and asks players to use available space.
- **Original cue:** Create another option before the ball arrives.
- **Original question:** Where could a teammate receive with room?
- **Board conditions:** A bounded possession area and two teams of six; use the foot-passing moment and retain the stated pass-total objective in the source details. Show opposing pressure and more than one possible option. Do not turn this into a fixed triangle, a 9v9 shape, or an unopposed sequence.

### A04 Help a target player

- **Source:** FIFA, [Playing the final pass](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/8-to-12/playing-the-final-pass.php), April 11, 2023; Skill development: 6v6, ball to captain, 2 goals. Ages **8–12**. Five players per side work centrally, with one target for each side in an end zone. Only the target scores; rotate that role after attempts.
- **Original cue:** Help the target become reachable.
- **Original question:** How can you create a clear route to the target?
- **Board conditions:** Three zones, twelve players total, both targets clearly identified, and goal direction. Explain the target's special role and rotation. Use the base activity; omit goalkeeper additions and the separate later game's heading variations. The precise marker positions are Sideline's schematic moment, not FIFA's prescribed coordinates.

### A05 See the second attacker

- **Source:** FIFA, [Developing core skills with Clearwater Chargers U-10](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/global-grassroots-insights/u-10-clearwater-core-skills-1v1.php), June 3, 2025; Part 2: 1v1 and 2v1 small-sided game, delivered by coach Juan de Brigard. Audience is explicitly **U-10**, not a generic U12 lesson.
- **Source conditions:** A 30×40m area, halfway division and two mini-goals at each end. An attacker engages the first defender; crossing halfway stops that defender and activates the second defender and a supporting attacker, creating **2v1**. The full sequence uses four players plus waiting players. [Part 2 instructions](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/global-grassroots-insights/u-10-clearwater-core-skills-1v1.php).
- **Original cue:** Look for the teammate who has joined the attack.
- **Original question:** What has changed now that you have support?
- **Board conditions:** Show the selected second-half 2v1 moment with boundaries, halfway and mini-goals; identify that the first defender is inactive behind the play. Explain the activation sequence rather than implying a generic permanent overload. Queues and live activation are omitted from the manual illustration.

## Reference candidates outside this pack

### A06 Defend a channel together

The FA's [week-two Defend as friends activity](https://learn.englandfootball.com/sessions/resources/2025/Marking-and-intercepting-session-defend-as-friends), June 9, 2025, contains a **3v2** channel: one attacker at each end and a third centrally, against two collaborating defenders. The [official session index](https://learn.englandfootball.com/sessions) identifies Vinny Halsall as an FA PE officer and the associated marking/intercepting programme as aimed at **7–11s**. The [programme](https://learn.englandfootball.com/sessions/resources/2025/Session-programme-marking-and-intercepting-to-defend) places this activity in week two. The page's metadata title incorrectly names another session; the visible activity and programme establish the reference.

**Original draft cue:** Work together to close passing routes. **Question:** What space should the second defender protect? A future schematic needs the narrow channel, end attackers and central attacker; it must retain 3v2 and must not imply a U12 full-team pressing scheme. This remains outside runtime pending separate reuse/link handling and adaptation review.

### A07 Win it then move into your area

The FA's [England DNA 5-11: staying on the ball](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/england-dna-5-11-staying-on-the-ball), April 4, 2019, explicitly targets **ages 5–11**. In its Organisation and How to play sections, paired opponents play **1v1** across two team-colored areas separated by a gap; the defender wins possession and takes the ball into the other area. The whole activity uses multiple pairs and balls.

**Original draft cue:** When you win it, carry it into your team's area. **Question:** Where can you keep the ball after winning it? A one-ball board could select one pair's moment but must still show both colored areas and explain the omitted simultaneous pairs. This remains outside runtime pending separate reuse/link handling and adaptation review.
